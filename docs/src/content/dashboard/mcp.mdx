# MCP Server

Betterlytics exposes your analytics data through a [Model Context Protocol (MCP)](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/) server. This lets you ask AI agents about your website's traffic in natural language, and connect real analytics data to your UI, UX, and code to make data-driven optimizations directly from your development workflow. No dashboards or manual queries needed.

## Connecting Your Agent

On Betterlytics Cloud, you connect an agent by adding the server URL to it and signing in. There's no token to create or paste:

1. Add `https://betterlytics.io/api/mcp` to your agent. Pick your client below for its steps.
2. Your browser opens Betterlytics. Sign in, or create an account if you don't have one yet.
3. Pick the dashboards the agent can read: **All dashboards**, which includes dashboards you add later, or **Only selected**.
4. Click **Connect**. The agent is connected, and reads those dashboards with your role on each.

An agent connected to several dashboards calls `list_dashboards` to see them, then reads the one your question is about.

Claude Desktop, Claude Code, Cursor, VS Code, Windsurf, Antigravity and Codex are supported. Each client's tab also offers **Use a personal token instead**, for a client that can't sign in.

<Callout type="info">
Self-hosting? Sign-in is only available on Betterlytics Cloud, so a self-hosted instance connects with a [personal access token](#personal-access-tokens). Your instance serves its own MCP endpoint at `/api/mcp`, for example `https://analytics.example.com/api/mcp`. Create the token on your instance under **Account settings > AI access**: the token is shown together with this address as the **Server URL**, with a copy button. **Dashboard > Settings > AI access** shows the Server URL too. Paste it into the **Server URL** field below, and the setup switches to the token snippets and install links for your instance.
</Callout>

## Personal Access Tokens

Use a personal access token for scripts and CI, for a client that can't sign in, or on a self-hosted instance. A token acts as you: it reads the dashboards you pick, with your current role on each, for as long as you're a member. Any member can create one, viewers included. Don't share it.

1. Open your **Account settings** and go to the **AI access** tab
2. Under **Personal access tokens**, click **Create token**
3. Enter a name for your token (e.g. "CI reports")
4. Pick the dashboards it can read
5. Choose an expiration: 30, 90, or 180 days (default 90), or no expiry
6. Click **Create token**
7. Copy the token immediately, it is only shown once

Tokens are read-only. Send the token as a bearer token (`Authorization: Bearer btl_...`); a token sent without the `Bearer ` prefix works too. To get a client's setup with the token filled in, click **Use a personal token instead** on its tab above and paste the token there.

## What You Can Ask About

Once connected, your AI assistant has access to your analytics data. You can query it conversationally, or let your agent use it to inform UI, UX, and code changes. Here are some of the things you can explore:

- **Traffic overview**: visitors, sessions, and pageviews over any time period
- **Engagement**: bounce rate, average visit duration, and pages per session, as totals or as a daily or hourly trend, matching the Overview
- **Landing and exit pages**: compare the pages where visits start and end by sessions, bounce rate, and visit duration
- **Page performance**: top pages, specific URL analysis, scroll depth
- **Devices and browsers**: desktop vs mobile vs tablet, browser breakdown
- **Geographic distribution**: visitor counts by country
- **Traffic sources**: where your visitors are coming from (search, social, direct, referrers)
- **UTM campaign tracking**: analyze traffic by source, medium, campaign, term, and content
- **Custom events**: track and analyze custom events you've set up
- **Trends over time**: daily or hourly breakdowns to spot patterns
- **Funnels**: list saved funnels with step-by-step conversion data, or run ad-hoc funnel analyses with custom steps
- **User journeys**: analyze navigation paths through your site, showing page-to-page transitions with traffic volumes
- **Error tracking**: list captured errors, inspect stack traces, and see the session trail leading up to each error
- **Uptime monitoring**: check whether your monitors are up right now, along with uptime percentage, response times, and SSL certificate status
- **Downtime incidents**: list ongoing and resolved outages with their severity, duration, and cause
- **Status pages**: see what your public status page shows visitors right now, and spot detected outages that have no posted incident

## Example Questions

Here are some questions you can try:

**Traffic analysis**
- "Compare my daily visitors this week vs last week. Is traffic trending up or down?"
- "How has my average visit duration changed day by day over the last 28 days?"
- "What percentage of my traffic comes from search vs social vs direct over the last 90 days?"
- "Which countries had the most visitors this month, and what devices do they use?"

**Content performance**
- "Which pages have the highest pageviews but lowest scroll depth? I might need to improve them."
- "Show me the top 10 landing pages by sessions, broken down by traffic source."
- "Which landing pages have the highest bounce rate this month?"
- "Where do visitors who land on /pricing end their visit?"
- "What's the average scroll depth on my blog posts vs my product pages?"

**Conversion and events**
- "How many cart-checkout events happened on mobile vs desktop this month?"
- "What's my product-clicked to cart-checkout ratio, and is it better on desktop or mobile?"
- "Show me a daily trend of custom events for the past 28 days, any spikes?"

**Campaign attribution**
- "Break down visitors by UTM campaign for Q1. Which campaigns drove the most traffic?"
- "How did traffic from my spring-sale UTM campaign perform compared to organic search?"
- "Which UTM campaigns bring visits with the lowest bounce rate and the longest visit duration?"

**Funnels**
- "How did my funnels perform last month?"
- "What's my signup conversion rate? Show me the drop-off at each step."
- "Run a funnel from /pricing to /signup to /dashboard. How many visitors make it through?"

**User journeys**
- "What are the most common navigation paths on my site?"
- "Show me user journeys from last month. Where do visitors go after the landing page?"
- "What pages do visitors explore after viewing the demo?"

**Error tracking**
- "What are the most common errors on my site this week?"
- "Show me the stack trace for the TypeError that's affecting the most users."
- "Where in my code is this error occurring?"
- "What was the user doing right before this error occurred? Walk me through the session trail."
- "Are there any new errors since my last deploy?"

**Uptime monitoring**
- "Are all my monitors up right now?"
- "What outages did we have this week, and how long did each one last?"
- "What's the uptime percentage for my API health check over the last 28 days?"
- "Are any of my SSL certificates expiring soon?"
- "Which monitor has the slowest response times this month?"

**Status pages**
- "What does my status page show visitors right now?"
- "Is there an ongoing outage that I haven't posted an incident for?"
- "Summarize the incidents currently published on my status page and their latest updates."

**Cross-dimensional**
- "What browsers do my mobile visitors from Germany use?"
- "Show me the hourly traffic pattern for today. When is my peak hour?"
- "Which referrer sources bring visitors that scroll the deepest on my pages?"

## Managing Connected Agents

On Betterlytics Cloud, agents you connected by signing in are listed under **Account settings > AI access**, in **Connected agents**. Each shows the dashboards it can read, when it connected, and when it was last used.

- **Edit dashboards** changes which dashboards an agent can read. It takes effect on the agent's next request, without reconnecting.
- **Revoke** ends the agent's access right away. To use it again, connect it again from the client.

## Managing Tokens

Personal access tokens are listed under **Account settings > AI access**, in **Personal access tokens**. Each token shows the dashboards it can read, its creation date, when it was last used, and when it expires (or that it never does). Use **Edit dashboards** to change which dashboards a token can read without changing the token itself. You can delete tokens at any time. Deleted tokens are immediately revoked.

Expired tokens stop authenticating and remain in the list so you can spot and delete them. A token's expiration can't be changed after creation; to extend access, create a new token.

## Dashboard AI Access

Every dashboard has an **AI access** page under **Dashboard > Settings > AI access**, with the Server URL and the **Allow AI agent access** switch. Every member sees whether it's on; only owners and admins can change it.

- **Off:** no agent connection or token can read the dashboard. Calls to it return an error saying its owners or admins turned AI access off, it's left out of `list_dashboards`, and members can't pick it for a new token or connection. Connections and tokens stay valid for their other dashboards.
- **On again:** existing connections and tokens read the dashboard again on their next request, without reconnecting.

Owners and admins also see every member's connection and token that can read the dashboard, and can remove the dashboard from any of them. Nothing is revoked: the connection or token keeps its other dashboards, an **All dashboards** connection keeps covering future dashboards, and the member can add this dashboard back by ticking it again under **Account settings > AI access**, where it shows as removed. To stop every agent, turn AI access off instead.

## Security

- Connected agents and tokens are read-only
- Each connected agent or token reads only the dashboards picked for it, with its owner's current role on each, and only while the dashboard's AI access is on
- A connected agent or token stops reading a dashboard on its next request once its owner leaves it, and stops working entirely if its owner's account is deleted or disabled
- Changing or resetting your password disconnects every agent you connected by signing in; connect them again afterwards. Personal access tokens keep working until you delete them or they expire
- Requests are rate-limited to 60 per minute per site
